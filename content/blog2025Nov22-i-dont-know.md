These phrases in a way define what it means to be a human being.

I don’t know. I give up. Let me get back to you.

GPT, for Generative Pre-trained Transformer, has improved greatly in the last few iterations. When prompted, it runs through several pre-trained algorithms to analyze the prompt first. Internally, it can rephrase the prompt and generate several graphs of questions and potential responses. It then predicts the best possible response.

However, currently, there is one flaw in all popular GPTs, Gemini, ChatGPT, and others. It is programmed to respond. This is where GPT hallucinates. Sometimes it does not have the data, and rather than responding with “I don’t have that data available”, it makes up the answer even though the probability of that answer being correct is next to zero.

Here is an example.

I asked two popular GPTs this question, a question to which I already had the answer:

> Does x use a or b?

It responded, “x uses a.”

It was the wrong answer, so I responded with “not true”, and then it responded with:

> You’re right to push back — there isn’t any clear public evidence that x exclusively uses a or b. My earlier statement was too definitive. Let’s clarify what’s actually known.

I had other instances in the past where GPT was completely off. I pushed back, and it backtracked.

Can the makers of GPTs address this? Yes, they can try by adding truth guardrails. But it is not that simple. Whose truth and which truth will it select to respond with? This is where the limitations of GPT are highlighted.

A human governed by principles and values would simply say, “I don’t know”, or “let me get back to you.”

GPT responses require human validation.

But GPT is the first chapter, and we are only midway through. The second is AGI. To unlock or start the AGI chapter, one key principle is required.
