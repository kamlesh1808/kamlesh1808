I found myself having discussions with my child regarding her career choices, in particular high school course selection and university program selection.

In the past years, I influenced the course selection. But now, by design, I am stepping back, asking questions, and learning more about what interests her, and more importantly, what does not.

My personal career choice was relatively easy. I had a high school Math and Computer Science teacher whose work influenced me.

Mr. V. taught me Grade 9 Math. In Grade 9, I learned BASIC programming language. In Grade 10, Pascal. Mr. V. taught us Visual Basic and Delphi in Grades 11 and 12. Yes, those programming languages are in the history books.

What excited me about a career in Computer Science was that Mr. V. had written a Math program to turn quadratic equation inputs into graphs that we could print on a dot-matrix printer. This was before Google.

I remember using the program in our Grade 12 Math class lab. I was amazed by the work. “One day I could build something like this,” I thought. But not a Math application; I knew this much very early on.

Thank you, Mr. V., for being there for me.
